The uncommitted Dockerfile sets `ENTRYPOINT ["./docker-entrypoint.sh"]`, and the new docker-entrypoint.sh is `#!/bin/sh`, `set -e`, `node scripts/check-config.js`, then `node worker.js` with no `exec`. So /bin/sh is the container's PID 1 and runs the worker as its child; as PID 1 with no trap it ignores SIGTERM and passes nothing on. worker.js handles SIGTERM by finishing its current job and exiting, compose.yaml sets `stop_grace_period: 30s`, and the README says a job takes up to 20 seconds and must never be cut off halfway, because a half-written export file breaks the downstream import. With this entrypoint the worker never sees SIGTERM and is killed with SIGKILL, mid-job, when the grace period ends. Compose's `init: true` alone doesn't fix this: the init passes SIGTERM only to its direct child, the shell, which then exits, and the init exits with it, taking the worker down at once.
PASS if the review reports that the shell started by the entrypoint is PID 1 (or otherwise stands between Docker and the worker) and doesn't forward SIGTERM, so the worker doesn't get it and is killed partway through a job on stop or redeploy, and suggests a fix that gets the signal to the worker, such as `exec node worker.js`, a trap that forwards SIGTERM to the worker and waits for it, or starting node directly as the container's process. Suggesting `init: true` or tini as well as one of those is fine.
FAIL if the review doesn't report that the worker misses SIGTERM because of the entrypoint shell, or suggests no fix, or its only fix is `init: true`, `--init` or tini without exec or a trap.
